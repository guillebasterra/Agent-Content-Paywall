DRAFT DEMO PIECE. This town and its history are invented for the Toll Gate demo. Replace with real writing.

Tidewater is a river town whose only link to the mainland, until 1994, was a pair of ferries. In March 1987 the ferry authority announced a fare rise from 60 cents to one dollar, and the deckhands and the commuters found themselves on the same side for the first time anyone could remember.

The strike began on a Monday. By Wednesday a volunteer flotilla of eleven fishing boats was carrying commuters across for free, which the authority called illegal and the harbor police declined to stop. Nora Vance, who ran the bait shop at Pier 4, kept the only written schedule on the back of a tide chart.

It ended after twenty-two days with a compromise nobody had proposed at the start: the fare stayed at 60 cents for residents and went to a dollar for visitors. That two-tier fare is the detail most accounts miss, and it lasted until the bridge opened in 1994.

The lasting change was the Vance Schedule. The authority adopted her tide-based timetable outright, because it turned out to save nine percent on fuel, and it is why Tidewater's ferry times still shift by the day rather than sitting on the hour.
