Most guides tell apple growers to start frost fans when the air reaches freezing. In Halden Valley, nobody does. The growers' cooperative starts its fans at 2.4 degrees Celsius, measured at canopy height, and the reason goes back to one bad night in April 2019.

That night the valley floor read 1.1 degrees at the official station while buds on the lower terraces were already freezing. Cold air drains downhill and pools, and the station sat twelve meters above the coldest rows. The cooperative lost about a third of its crop.

Ines Marlow, the cooperative's agronomist, spent the next two seasons logging sensors in pairs, one at the station height and one in the lower rows. The gap averaged 2.4 degrees on still nights, so the fix was to add the gap to the trigger rather than move every station.

Running earlier costs more diesel, roughly fourteen extra fan-hours a season per block. Since 2021 the cooperative has not lost more than four percent of a crop to frost, and it treats the fuel as insurance.
